How to read these comparisons

Known-mode binary phase-shift keying (BPSK) is detected by maximum likelihood. The formal data use the isotropic AVS noise model: the total noise energy of the three pressure-equivalent velocity components equals that of the pressure channel. Normalization and channel calibration are experiment specific and recorded in each source table.

The SNR sweep includes the manuscript’s pressure nonorthogonality model (mixing parameter ε = 0.35). Offset experiments compare a nominal detector with a detector calibrated at the actual offset. For distance, the nominal SNR is 0 dB at z₀ = 1.2 m; the normalization is fixed at z₀. Pilot experiments use 5 dB for training and data. The hardware view exposes the sampled phase-error grid for a chosen amplitude error and SNR; it does not interpolate a threshold surface.

These results use monochromatic free-field propagation and ideal Cartesian sensor responses. They establish the modeled reception mechanism and its dependence on sampling, noise, calibration and hardware errors. Broadband propagation, multipath and measured sensor responses are extensions of this framework.
